Racoon Holiday

Holiday Poem

Masked little bandits with bandit-striped tails, Trudging through dust on the desert-hot trails. We left all the trash cans of home far behind, To see what sweet wonders the West had in kind. The midnight freight train carried steel and our crew, Across the wide river as cool breezes blew. We hopped off in Flagstaff where ponderosas rise, Under crisp mountain moonlight and shimmering skies. Pip cracked open a cooler stuffed full of gourmet, While Silas washed gouda to start off the day. We hitched a quick ride on a tour bus roof-top, And made the red rocks of Sedona our stop. While humans sat cross-legged chasing their dreams, We hunted for churros and sugary creams. Barnaby wrestled a turquoise display, 'Til a loud shriek from a raven scared us away! We trotted down south as the shadows grew long, Where crickets were singing their evening song. We scampered past saguaros reaching up high, Like giant green prickly arms touching the sky. We stood on the rim of the canyon so vast, Where shadow and sunlight danced purple and fast. In Phoenix we found a resort pool after dark, And treated the deck like our personal park. Big Silas swam loops on a bright yellow noodle, While Pip polished off a sweet tiki bar puddle. Barnaby perched on a rooftop to stare At the city lights sparkling through desert-warm air. Through red rocks of Sedona and canyons of red, We wandered all day till we tumbled to bed. With pockets of soda tabs, copper, and stone, We found a cool wash to call home of our own. With dust on our paws and the sun in our eyes, Arizona's the home of the best raccoon skies!

More about the trip

The midnight freight out of Needles dropped three vagabond raccoons into the dry, electric air of Arizona. Barnaby had the plan, Pip had the appetite, and big Silas was along for the snacks. Their adventure began in the crisp ponderosa pines of Flagstaff, where Pip cracked open a camper’s abandoned cooler to score smoked gouda and salted cashews. By morning, they were riding the roof of a southbound tour bus into Sedona’s red rocks. While humans sat cross-legged chasing "vortices," Silas scored cinnamon churros outside a bakery, and Barnaby tried—and failed—to pry a turquoise stone from a sidewalk display. Following Pip’s sugar-seeking nose, they descended into the Sonoran Desert by nightfall, weaving past towering saguaros to hit the ultimate prize: a resort pool deck in Phoenix. Silas drifted peacefully on a floating foam noodle, Pip splashed in leftover pineapple syrup at the tiki bar, and Barnaby gazed out over the shimmering city lights. As dawn warmed the horizon, the trio retreated into the shadow of a desert wash, pockets bulging with soda tabs and Sedona stone, ready to rest up for the next raid.
